NOTSUOH · early in the evening

The building gets weirder the farther I go

Walking into NOTSUOH feels less like entering a bar than wandering into somebody's collection after they've spent thirty years refusing to throw anything away. The first floor is packed with old furniture, mannequins, art, lamps, chandeliers, antiques and taxidermy. There is a bar toward the back, but my eyes keep getting pulled somewhere else.

Then I go upstairs. The second level gets smaller and more intimate, with another bar tucked into it and places to actually sit and talk. From there a metal spiral staircase carries me up again, and the third floor opens into another world: gallery rooms, private little lounging spaces, books and objects everywhere, and the Dome sitting inside all of it. It feels like the building keeps rewarding me for exploring it.

Before the performance, I'm looking at the work and talking with one of the artists. I remember overhearing him describe the paintings in intensely personal terms: his illness, a dream, the work as a physical expression of what he has lived through. That part survives in my memory rather than a clean transcript, so I'm not going to pretend I can quote it. What Fieldy does catch clearly is the wonderfully mundane answer when I ask how he lands on the black, white and red enamel palette.

“So how did you land on this process with the enamel on the white black and the red?”, Scott · NOTSUOH · ~9:47 PM
“They were the only colors available to me from Home Depot.”, Artist · NOTSUOH · ~9:47 PM

Current frontier

One more question

By Saturday the NOW path is no longer just an idea. The live execution path works, the harness has 98 passing tests in the latest verified development state, and a state-changing run can leave durable evidence, reconcile project state, throw away the original controller, and reconstruct correctly in a fresh process. So naturally the next problem appears: multiple workers and projects now need one trustworthy operational surface, and they should wake because something became ready—not merely because an hour passed. The Command Center is turning from a dashboard into that control surface. I find one thing that annoys me or seems possible, ask one more question, and follow whatever breaks next.

“In order to maintain the fluidity that we have now, but also have the added benefit of being able to have conversations in between without interrupting task execution.”, Scott · Friday afternoon
Engineering epilogue

What exists now

What exists now is not really one app. I still talk to Sol basically the same way I do in the car Wednesday night. The difference is what now sits underneath the conversation: repos and project packets that hold durable evidence, persistent project lanes, a proven NOW execution path, machine-checkable reconciliation that refuses to trust stale project state, and a Command Center rollout intended to show what is genuinely running, handed off, ready, blocked, waiting on me, stale, or stopped. The architecture is beginning to shift toward event-driven readiness while hourly dispatchers remain the safety net. Direct Slack delivery works; scheduled-worker → Slack delivery is still being proved, so the system does not get to call that solved yet.

There is no point where I decide to build an operating system. I just keep asking what happens if we go one step further.
